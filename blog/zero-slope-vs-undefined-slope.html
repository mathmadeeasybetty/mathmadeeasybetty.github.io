Zero Slope vs. Undefined Slope: What's the Difference?

A horizontal line has zero slope. A vertical line has undefined slope. The difference comes down to one question: where is the zero in the slope formula?

November 21, 2026
Try it first

Evaluate these three fractions. Which one equals zero? Which ones are undefined?

$\dfrac{0}{5}\qquad \dfrac{5}{0}\qquad \dfrac{0}{0}$

Show the answer

$\dfrac{0}{5}=0$

$\dfrac{5}{0}$ is undefined, and $\dfrac{0}{0}$ is also undefined.

Zero in the numerator is not the same as zero in the denominator. The third fraction needs a closer look, too.

These three fractions explain the difference between zero slope and undefined slope. Many students memorize that horizontal lines have zero slope and vertical lines have undefined slope, but never learn why. The reason is where the zero sits in the fraction: on top or on the bottom.

01 · What slope tells you

Slope measures how steep a line is and which way it leans. Read a graph from left to right, the way you read a sentence. If the line climbs, the slope is positive. If it drops, the slope is negative.

The sign gives the direction and the size gives the steepness. A slope of $3$ climbs faster than a slope of $\tfrac{1}{2}$, and $-3$ drops faster than $-\tfrac{1}{2}$.

Positive and negative slopes are easy to see. The last two pictures, flat and vertical, are where students mix things up. That is the focus of this guide.

02 · Start with the slope formula

Slope compares the vertical change with the horizontal change between two points. It is often called rise over run.

$\displaystyle m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$

The numerator, $y_2-y_1$, measures the change in $y$ (the rise). The denominator, $x_2-x_1$, measures the change in $x$ (the run).

Notice the order: vertical change divided by horizontal change. The subtraction order must also match in the numerator and denominator.

Example 1: a rising line. Use $(1,2)$ and $(4,8)$.

$\displaystyle m=\frac{8-2}{4-1}=\frac{6}{3}=2$

The slope is positive: the line climbs $2$ units for every $1$ unit to the right.

Example 2: a falling line. Use $(-1,5)$ and $(3,1)$.

$\displaystyle m=\frac{1-5}{3-(-1)}=\frac{-4}{4}=-1$

The slope is negative: the line drops $1$ unit for every $1$ unit to the right.

Order check. It does not matter which point you call $(x_1,y_1)$ and which you call $(x_2,y_2)$, as long as you use the same order on top and on the bottom. In Example 1, starting from $(4,8)$ instead of $(1,2)$ gives $\dfrac{2-8}{1-4}=\dfrac{-6}{-3}=2$, the same answer. Mixing the order, as in $\dfrac{8-2}{1-4}$, gives $-2$, which is wrong.

03 · Zero slope: a horizontal line

A horizontal line does not go up or down. Its two points have the same $y$-coordinate, so the rise is zero. But the points have different $x$-coordinates, so the run is not zero.

Use the points $(1,2)$ and $(5,2)$:

$\begin{aligned} m&=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\\[4pt] &=\frac{2-2}{5-1}\\[4pt] &=\frac{0}{4}\\[4pt] &=0 \end{aligned}$

The denominator is nonzero, so the fraction is defined. Zero divided by a nonzero number equals zero.

A horizontal line has a slope of zero. Zero is the actual value of its slope; it does not mean the slope is missing.

Real-life picture: a level hallway. Imagine walking straight along a perfectly level hallway. Your horizontal position changes, but your height stays the same. That is zero rise and a nonzero run, so the slope is zero.

04 · Undefined slope: a vertical line

A vertical line does not move left or right. Its two points have the same $x$-coordinate, so the run is zero. The points have different $y$-coordinates, so the rise is nonzero.

Use the points $(3,1)$ and $(3,5)$:

$\begin{aligned} m&=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\\[4pt] &=\frac{5-1}{3-3}\\[4pt] &=\frac{4}{0} \end{aligned}$

Division by zero is undefined. There is no number you can multiply by zero to get $4$.

A vertical line has an undefined slope. It is not zero. The exact mathematical answer is undefined, not infinity.

Real-life picture: an elevator. Imagine an elevator moving straight up inside a vertical shaft. Let $x$ represent its horizontal position and $y$ represent its height. The elevator stays 4 meters from a chosen horizontal reference, while its height changes from 2 meters to 12 meters. Its positions are $(4,2)$ and $(4,12)$.

The elevator rises 10 meters, but its horizontal position does not change:

$\displaystyle m=\frac{10}{0}$

Because division by zero is undefined, the elevator's slope is undefined.

Why can't this quotient be a number? Suppose its slope were some real number $m$. The relationship between rise and run would require:

$10=m\times 0$

But every real number multiplied by zero equals zero, never 10. No real number can satisfy this equation. That is what undefined slope means here: there is no real-number value for the rise-per-run ratio when the run is zero.

A vertical line is not assigned an infinite slope. Slopes can become very large in magnitude as a line gets closer to vertical, but the slope of the exact vertical line is undefined.

The Trap

Switching zero slope and undefined slope

Common mistake

Horizontal line: no rise, so slope is undefined.

Vertical line: no run, so slope is zero.

Correct reasoning

Horizontal: $\dfrac{0}{4}=0$.

Vertical: $\dfrac{4}{0}$ is undefined.

The mistake comes from noticing that one change is zero but not checking whether it is the numerator or the denominator.

05 · Why is $\dfrac{0}{0}$ different?

What happens if you use the same point twice, such as $(2,5)$ and $(2,5)$?

$m=\dfrac{5-5}{2-2}=\dfrac{0}{0}$

This does not give a slope. You have used one point twice, not two distinct points that determine a line. Many different lines can pass through a single point, so one point alone does not determine a unique slope.

There is also a way to see why the three fractions behave differently. Division asks: what number multiplied by the denominator gives the numerator?

$\begin{aligned} 5q=0 &\implies q=0 \\[6pt] 0q=5 &\implies \text{no solution} \\[6pt] 0q=0 &\implies \text{every }q\text{ works} \end{aligned}$

For $\dfrac{0}{5}$, there is one answer: zero. For $\dfrac{5}{0}$, there is no answer. For $\dfrac{0}{0}$, there is no single answer because every number would work. That is why $\dfrac{0}{0}$ is not equal to zero.

06 · Keep the three cases separate

Use the numerator and denominator to identify what the slope formula gives you:

  1. Zero numerator, nonzero denominator: the slope is zero. The line is horizontal. $\dfrac{0}{4}=0$
  2. Nonzero numerator, zero denominator: the slope is undefined. The line is vertical. $\dfrac{4}{0}$ is undefined.
  3. Both numerator and denominator are zero: the slope cannot be determined from the same point repeated. $\dfrac{0}{0}$ is undefined.

Practice: zero, undefined, or not enough information?

Find each slope. Decide what the result means before opening the solution.

Find the slope

A. A line passes through $(-2,4)$ and $(3,4)$.

$m=\dfrac{?}{?}$

Show the solution

The $y$-coordinates are equal, so the rise is zero:

$\displaystyle m=\frac{4-4}{3-(-2)}=\frac{0}{5}=0$

The slope is zero. The line is horizontal.

B. A line passes through $(4,-1)$ and $(4,6)$.

$m=\dfrac{?}{?}$

Show the solution

The $x$-coordinates are equal, so the run is zero:

$\displaystyle m=\frac{6-(-1)}{4-4}=\frac{7}{0}$

The slope is undefined. The line is vertical.

Explain the difference

C. A student says, "A horizontal line has no rise, so its slope is undefined." What is the error?

Show the solution

The student is confusing a zero numerator with a zero denominator. A horizontal line has zero rise but a nonzero run, so its slope is $\dfrac{0}{\text{nonzero}}=0$.

D. Can you find the slope using the point $(2,5)$ twice?

Show the solution

No. Repeating the same point gives $\dfrac{0}{0}$. One point does not determine a unique line, so there is not enough information to find a slope.

E. A student finds the slope through $(1,2)$ and $(4,8)$ and writes $m=\dfrac{8-2}{1-4}=-2$. What went wrong? What is the correct slope?

Show the solution

The top starts with $(4,8)$, but the bottom starts with $(1,2)$. Both must use the same order.

$\displaystyle m=\frac{8-2}{4-1}=\frac{6}{3}=2$

The correct slope is $2$. Sign check: the line goes up from $(1,2)$ to $(4,8)$, so the slope must be positive.

Mixed practice

F. A line passes through $(5,3)$ and $(1,7)$. Find the slope. Does the line rise or fall?

Show the solution

$\displaystyle m=\frac{7-3}{1-5}=\frac{4}{-4}=-1$

The slope is $-1$. It is negative, so the line falls from left to right.

G. What is the slope of the line $y=7$? What is the slope of the line $x=-2$?

Show the solution

Every point on $y=7$ has height $7$. Take $(0,7)$ and $(3,7)$: $\dfrac{7-7}{3-0}=0$. The slope is zero.

Every point on $x=-2$ has $x$-coordinate $-2$. Take $(-2,1)$ and $(-2,4)$: $\dfrac{4-1}{-2-(-2)}=\dfrac{3}{0}$. The slope is undefined.

Quick checklist

  1. Horizontal line: zero rise and nonzero run. Slope = $0$.
  2. Vertical line: nonzero rise and zero run. Slope = undefined.
  3. Same point twice: zero rise and zero run. The slope cannot be determined.
  4. Equation $y=c$: a horizontal line. Slope = $0$.
  5. Equation $x=c$: a vertical line. Slope = undefined.

Remember: Zero is defined; division by zero is not. In the slope formula, where zero appears matters!
