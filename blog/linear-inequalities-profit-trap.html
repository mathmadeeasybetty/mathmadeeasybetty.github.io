Over What Interval Is Store A's Profit Greater Than Store B's?

Two stores, two profit formulas, and one question that has a surprising answer. Solve it, then check whether your answer makes sense in the real world.

November 1, 2026

Store A's profit is modeled by f(x) = 3x, and Store B's profit is modeled by g(x) = 9x. Over what interval is Store A's profit greater than Store B's?

Store A: $f(x) = 3x$    Store B: $g(x) = 9x$

It is tempting to glance at $9x$ and $3x$ and answer "$x > 0$." That answer is wrong, and for two separate reasons. Let us solve it properly.

Try it first

Before reading the solution, one quick question. Let $x = -1$.

Which is greater, $f(-1)$ or $g(-1)$?

Show the answer

$f(-1) = 3(-1) = -3$ and $g(-1) = 9(-1) = -9$, so $f$ is greater, because $-3 > -9$.

Store A only wins for negative $x$. That one fact is the whole problem.

Method 1: Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Write the inequality. Store A's profit is greater when $f(x) > g(x)$:

$3x > 9x$

Step 2: Get the $x$ terms on one side. Subtract $9x$ from both sides:

$3x - 9x > 0$
$-6x > 0$

Step 3: Divide by $-6$. Dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign, so $>$ becomes $<$:

$x < 0$

Step 4: Check with test values. Never skip this. Try one number on each side of $0$:

  • $x = -1$: $f(-1) = -3$ and $g(-1) = -9$. Since $-3 > -9$, Store A is greater. ✓
  • $x = 1$: $f(1) = 3$ and $g(1) = 9$. Since $3 > 9$ is false, Store A is not greater. ✓

So algebraically, Store A's profit is greater than Store B's when $x < 0$. That is the interval $(-\infty,\, 0)$.

Method 2: Avoid the Sign-Flip Error

Worried you will forget to flip the sign? Use this method instead. Move the $x$ terms to the side where the coefficient stays positive, so you never divide by a negative number.

Step 1: Start with the inequality.

$3x > 9x$

Step 2: Subtract $3x$ from both sides, so the $x$ term ends up on the right with a positive coefficient:

$0 > 6x$

Step 3: Read it from the right. $0 > 6x$ means the same as $6x < 0$. Now divide by $6$, a positive number, so the sign stays exactly as it is:

$x < 0$

Same answer, and there was nothing to flip. Rule of thumb: if the $x$ coefficient would become negative, move the $x$ terms to the other side first.

The Real-World Domain Trap

Both methods give $x < 0$, but this is a store, not just a pair of equations. Here $x$ stands for something you can count, such as items sold or days open. You cannot sell a negative number of items, so the domain is $x > 0$. (If your textbook defines $x$ differently, use its domain instead.)

Now compare the two conditions. Store A wins only when $x < 0$. The store can only operate when $x > 0$. No number satisfies both.

Algebraic solution: $(-\infty, 0)$.

Feasible real-world solution: $\varnothing$ (no values). For every positive selling quantity, Store B earns more than Store A; at zero, their modeled profits are equal.

This also makes sense without algebra. Store B earns $9$ for every $3$ that Store A earns, so Store B is ahead from the first sale.

Error Analysis: Two Ways Students Lose Marks

The Trap

Forgetting to flip the sign, then trusting the "nice" answer

Wrong way

$-6x > 0$
$x > 0$
"Store A wins for all $x > 0$"

Fixed

$-6x > 0$
$x < 0$ (sign flipped)
Domain is $x > 0$, so no interval

Mistake 1: not flipping the sign. Dividing by $-6$ without reversing the sign gives $x > 0$. That result can look plausible because it matches the selling domain, but it fails a direct check: at $x=1$, Store A earns $3$ and Store B earns $9$. The two methods above show how to avoid the error.

Mistake 2: stopping at $x < 0$. This is the correct algebraic solution, but it is outside the feasible domain. Intersect the two sets before stating the real-world answer: here, the intersection is empty.

Your turn · Practice

Five quick checks. Try each one on paper first, then press the + to see the answer.

1. Swap the stores. Over what interval is Store B's profit greater than Store A's? Use the domain $x > 0$.

$9x > 3x$

Show the answer

$9x - 3x > 0$
$6x > 0$
$x > 0$

You divide by a positive number ($6$), so the sign does not flip. The answer $x > 0$ is inside the domain, so Store B is greater for every $x > 0$, the interval $(0,\, \infty)$.

2. Solve. This one needs the flip.

$-5x > 20$

Show the answer

$x \,<\, -4$

Dividing both sides by $-5$ reverses the sign. Check: at $x = -5$, $-5(-5) = 25$, and $25 > 20$ is true.

3. Use Method 2, then check the domain. Store C earns $2x$ and Store D earns $7x$, with $x > 0$. Over what interval is Store C greater?

$2x > 7x$

Show the answer

$2x > 7x$
$0 > 5x$
$5x < 0$
$x < 0$

The domain is $x > 0$, so there is no interval. Store C is never greater.

4. Spot the mistake. A student solved this inequality. Where did the work go wrong?

$-3x < 12$
$x < -4$

Show the answer

The student divided by $-3$ but did not flip the sign. The correct answer is $x > -4$.

Check: at $x = 0$, $-3(0) = 0$ and $0 < 12$ is true, and $0 > -4$ matches. At $x = -5$, $-3(-5) = 15$ and $15 < 12$ is false, so $x = -5$ is not a solution.

5. A bounded interval. Store E earns $2x + 6$ (it has a $6$ unit head start) and Store F earns $5x$, with $x > 0$. Over what interval is Store E greater?

$2x + 6 > 5x$

Show the answer

$2x + 6 > 5x$
$6 > 3x$
$2 > x$
$x < 2$

Combine with the domain $x > 0$ to get $0 < x < 2$, the interval $(0,\, 2)$. Check: at $x = 1$, $8 > 5$ is true. At $x = 3$, $12 > 15$ is false.

The habit to keep is simple: solve, flip the sign when you divide by a negative, test one value, then check the domain. Four quick checks, and the trap has nowhere to hide.
