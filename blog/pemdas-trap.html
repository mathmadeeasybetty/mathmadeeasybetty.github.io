Does Multiplication Come Before Division? The PEMDAS Trap That Caught 59% of People

167 people answered this order of operations question in a math poll. 59% got it wrong, even with a hint. Here is exactly where they lost it.

August 28, 2026 · 2 min read
Try it first

167 people were given this in a math poll. 59% got it wrong, even with a hint in the question. See how you do before you read on.

$-6 + (1 + 3^3) \div 2 \times 7$

Show the answer

The answer is $\mathbf{92}$.

If you got $-4$, you are in the 59%, and you are in good company. The rest of this post is about the single step where that goes wrong.

Short answer: no. Neither one outranks the other. Multiplication and division sit on the same level, and you work them left to right, whichever appears first. The mistake that caught most of that 59% comes from how the acronym itself is taught.

Most people read PEMDAS (Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication, Division, Addition, Subtraction) as six steps in order. It is really four. Multiplication and Division share one level and are worked left to right. Addition and Subtraction share the level below, also left to right.

If you were taught BODMAS or BIDMAS instead, nothing changes. The letters are shuffled and Order or Indices replaces Exponents, but the four levels and the left-to-right rule are identical. Any acronym that appears to put division ahead of multiplication, or addition ahead of subtraction, is an artifact of spelling, not a rule of arithmetic.

That is not an arbitrary rule to memorise. Dividing by $2$ is the same as multiplying by $\frac{1}{2}$, and subtracting $6$ is the same as adding $-6$. They are the same operation wearing a different sign, so neither can outrank the other. All you can do is take them in the order they appear.

So once you work out $(1 + 3^3) = 28$, the question becomes $-6 + 28 \div 2 \times 7$, and this is where people split:

The Trap

Treating multiplication as higher priority than division

Wrong way

$2 \times 7 = 14$
$-6 + 28 \div 14$
$-6 + 2 = -4$

Fixed

$28 \div 2 = 14$
$-6 + 14 \times 7$
$14 \times 7 = 98$
$-6 + 98 = 92$

The same trap, one row down

Addition and Subtraction share their own level and follow the identical rule, which is where the other half of the mistake happens. The A in the acronym does not outrank the S.

Your turn

Work this one out on paper before you open the answer.

$20 - 4 + 6$

Show the answer

Subtraction and addition sit on the same level, so you take them left to right.

$20 - 4 = 16$, then $16 + 6 = \mathbf{22}$.

If you got $10$, you did $4 + 6$ first because A comes before S in PEMDAS. That is the same mistake as the one above, one row down. Neither operation outranks the other, so the left one goes first.

Reading the rule makes sense on paper. Catching yourself breaking it under exam pressure is a different skill, and it only comes from practice. You learn math by doing math.
