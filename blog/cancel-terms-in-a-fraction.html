Can You Cancel the x in (x + 3)/(x + 4)?

By the end of this article, you will know why the x in this fraction cannot be canceled, when a common factor can be canceled, and how to test a shortcut.

November 14, 2026
Try it first

Can the $x$ in the numerator and denominator be canceled to get $\frac{3}{4}$?

$\dfrac{x+3}{x+4}$

Show the answer

The short answer is No.

If you thought you could cancel, this is a very common algebra mistake. The rest of this article shows why it fails, and when canceling is allowed.

01 · How to test a cancellation shortcut

A valid algebraic equality must hold for every allowed value of $x$.

$\dfrac{x+3}{x+4}$ is defined for every $x$ except $-4$, because that value makes the denominator zero. Pick an easy number to test the claim. Let $x = 1$.

  • Top: $1 + 3 = 4$
  • Bottom: $1 + 4 = 5$
  • The fraction is $\frac{4}{5}$

But the canceled answer says $\frac{3}{4}$. Since $\frac{4}{5} \ne \frac{3}{4}$, the shortcut is false. One counterexample is enough to show that it does not work in general.

Avoid $x = 0$ for this test. The original fraction and the canceled result both happen to equal $\frac{3}{4}$, but one matching value does not prove that the shortcut is valid.

02 · Factors vs. Terms: what you can and can't cancel

Look at this example:

$\dfrac{3x}{3}$

Here, $3x$ means $3\cdot x$. The $3$ and $x$ are factors because they are multiplied. The 3 is a common factor of the entire numerator and denominator, so it can be canceled:

$\dfrac{3x}{3}=\dfrac{3\cdot x}{3}=x$

Why Does Canceling a Factor Work? Because any nonzero number divided by itself equals 1.

\[ \frac{3}{3}=1 \]

So when we cancel the common factor in

\[ \frac{3\cdot x}{3} \]

we are simply replacing it with 1:

\[ 1\cdot x=x \]

Canceling is a shortcut for pulling out a factor that equals $1$. That is why the factor must be nonzero: $\dfrac{0}{0}$ is undefined, not $1$.

Now compare it with this expression:

$\dfrac{x+3}{x+4}$

In the numerator, $x$ and $3$ are terms because they are joined by a plus sign. The same is true of $x$ and $4$ in the denominator. Cancellation works with common factors that multiply the entire numerator and denominator, not with individual terms in a sum. Here, $x$ is only one part of each sum, not a factor of the entire numerator or denominator.

Common Mistake: Mixing terms with factors.

You can only cancel a nonzero common factor of the entire numerator and the entire denominator, never a single piece of a sum or difference (a term).

The Trap

Canceling a piece of a sum

Common mistake

Canceling the 3.
$\dfrac{3+6}{3+9} = \dfrac{6}{9}$

Correct way

Factor first, then simplify.
$\dfrac{3(1+2)}{3(1+3)} = \dfrac{3}{4}$

03 · When can you cancel in a fraction?

Cancel only a nonzero common factor of the entire numerator and the entire denominator. A factor can be a number, a variable, or a whole expression in parentheses.

Here, $x$ is a common factor of the entire numerator and denominator:

$\dfrac{x(x+3)}{x} = \dfrac{x \cdot (x+3)}{x \cdot 1} = x+3, \quad x\ne0$

But in $\dfrac{x+3}{x+4}$, the $x$ is just one term in each sum. It is not a common factor of the numerator and denominator.

A whole expression in parentheses can also be a common factor:

$\dfrac{5(x+2)}{3(x+2)} = \dfrac{5}{3}$

The entire expression $(x+2)$ is a factor of both the numerator and the denominator, so it cancels. We cancel the whole expression, not just the $x$ inside it. This works for every $x$ except $-2$, because that would make the denominator zero.

04 · How to simplify a fraction with a sum on top

Factor the numerator, then look for a factor the denominator also has. Factoring means rewriting a sum as a product. For example, both terms in $2x + 6$ share a factor of $2$, so $2x + 6 = 2(x+3)$. The denominator is also $2$, so there are two ways to simplify $\dfrac{2x+6}{2}$.

Method 1: Divide each term by 2.

$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{2x+6}{2} &= \dfrac{2x}{2}+\dfrac{6}{2}\\ &= x+3 \end{aligned}$

Method 2: Factor out 2 first.

$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{2x+6}{2} &= \dfrac{2(x+3)}{2}\\ &= x+3 \end{aligned}$

Another slip to watch for is dividing only the first term:

The Trap

Dividing only one term

Common mistake

$\dfrac{2x+6}{2} = x + 6$

Correct way

$\dfrac{2x+6}{2} = x + 3$

05 · Practice in three steps

Complete each exercise before opening the corresponding solution.

Step 1 · Test the equality

A. Determine whether the equality is correct. Evaluate the numerator first.

$\dfrac{5+3}{5} = 3$

Show the solution

Evaluate the numerator, then compare the result with 3:

$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{5+3}{5} &= \dfrac{8}{5}\\ &= 1.6 \neq 3 \end{aligned}$

The equality is incorrect. The 5 in the numerator is added to 3, so it is not a common factor that can be canceled.

B. Substitute $x = 4$ and determine whether the equality holds.

$\dfrac{2+x}{2} = x$

Show the solution

Substitute $x = 4$ into both sides:

$\begin{aligned} \text{Left side} &= \dfrac{2+4}{2} = 3\\ \text{Right side} &= 4 \end{aligned}$

Since $3 \neq 4$, the equality is incorrect. The 2 is added to $x$, so it cannot be canceled.

Step 2 · Identify common factors

C. Simplify the fraction. Identify the common factor.

$\dfrac{4x}{4}$

Show the solution

Rewrite the numerator as a product to identify the common factor:

$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{4x}{4} &= \dfrac{4\cdot x}{4}\\ &= x \end{aligned}$

The simplified answer is $x$. The 4 is a common factor of the entire numerator and denominator.

D. Identify the common factor and simplify.

$\dfrac{5(x+2)}{3(x+2)}$

Show the solution

Identify the common factor multiplied on both the top and bottom:

$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{5(x+2)}{3(x+2)} &= \dfrac{5}{3} \end{aligned}$

The simplified answer is $\frac{5}{3}$. The entire factor $(x+2)$ can be canceled. This is valid for $x \neq -2$, because the original denominator cannot equal zero.

Step 3 · Factor and simplify

E. Factor the numerator, then simplify.

$\dfrac{2x+6}{2}$

Show the solution

Find the common factor of both terms in the numerator, then rewrite and simplify:

$\begin{aligned} 2x+6 &= 2(x+3)\\[8pt] \dfrac{2x+6}{2} &= \dfrac{2(x+3)}{2}\\[8pt] &= x+3 \end{aligned}$

The simplified answer is $x+3$. Factoring makes the common factor visible so it can be canceled.

Quick checklist

  1. Individual terms in a sum or difference: Do not cancel a single term. $\dfrac{x+3}{x+4} \neq \dfrac{3}{4}$
  2. Common factors on top and bottom: Cancel only a factor of the entire numerator and denominator. A whole expression in parentheses can be a factor. $\dfrac{5(x+2)}{3(x+2)} = \dfrac{5}{3}$
  3. A sum on top: Factor it first, then cancel any factor the bottom shares. $\dfrac{2x+6}{2} = \dfrac{2(x+3)}{2} = x + 3$
